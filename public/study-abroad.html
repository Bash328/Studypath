Studying abroad, honestly

Studying overseas is a real option – and the area with the most confident-sounding wrong information online. This page explains how the process works and what to check.

Why there are no entry requirements on this page

Everywhere else on Studypath, every requirement links to the official page it came from. We haven’t verified any foreign university’s requirements, so rather than repeat what agents and forums say, we’ve left them out.

Everything below is ! general guidance about the process – each paragraph is marked, and each section tells you where to check it.

🛤️

Two ways to do it

🌍 Route 1 – Apply straight to a foreign university

You apply as an international applicant, straight from matric, and do your whole degree overseas. This gives you the most choice and costs the most.

! Not from an official source

It usually involves: an application through that country’s system (some countries have one central service, others have you apply to each university); having your NSC assessed against that country’s qualifications; sometimes an English test; a student visa that normally needs proof you can pay; and deadlines that can be a year earlier than you expect.

! Not from an official source

Where to check this yourself
  • The university’s own page for international applicants
  • The official student-visa page of the country’s government

🔁 Route 2 – Start in South Africa, go abroad part-way

You register for a degree here, then spend a semester or a year at a partner university overseas through an exchange or study-abroad agreement. Your home university still gives you the degree.

! Not from an official source

People often prefer this because it is usually far cheaper (you keep paying South African fees), you apply using your university results, and the degree stays a South African one. Which partners exist, and who qualifies, differs by university and faculty.

! Not from an official source

Where to check this yourself
  • Your university’s international office
  • Our Ask a university page (choose “I am an international student” for one example contact)
🗓️

What to line up, and roughly when

This is a rough order, not a rule – every country and university has its own calendar.

! Not from an official source

WhenWhat to sort out
Grade 11Shortlist countries and universities. Find each one’s page for international applicants and write down its deadline.
Grade 11Work out the real total cost: tuition, living costs, visa, flights, medical cover – and the rand exchange rate over three or four years.
Early Grade 12Book any admission or language tests; some sittings fill up. Apply – many northern-hemisphere deadlines fall while you’re still in Grade 12.
Grade 12Apply to South African universities too. An overseas plan with no backup is not a plan.
After resultsQualification assessment, visa application, proof of funds, accommodation.
🤔

Questions to ask before you commit

Will the degree be recognised back home?

If you plan to work in South Africa in a regulated profession – medicine, engineering, law, teaching, accounting – check with that profession’s registration body before you enrol, not after you graduate.

! Not from an official source

What does it really cost?

Compare the full multi-year total against the same degree here, including the rand weakening. International student fees are usually much higher than local ones.

! Not from an official source

Can you work while studying?

Student-visa work rules vary by country. Plans that depend on part-time income can fall apart if the visa doesn’t allow it.

! Not from an official source

Who is advising you?

Some agents are paid commission by the universities they recommend. That doesn’t make them dishonest, but verify everything on the university’s own site.

! Not from an official source

Where to check this yourself
  • The registration body for your profession
  • The destination university’s official fees page
  • The destination country’s official student-visa page

🛡️ A note on scams

Nobody legitimate guarantees admission, and nobody legitimate asks for a large payment to “secure your place” before the university has made you an offer in writing. If a claim can’t be found on the university’s own website, treat it as false.

! Not from an official source
