The test nobody explains to you

The NBT, in plain language

If a university has told you to "write your NBTs", this page explains what that actually is, whether you need to do it, and what score each university is looking for.

What the NBT is

NBT stands for National Benchmark Tests. They're written tests that some South African universities make you write in addition to matric. They don't test the school syllabus — they test how well you read academic writing, how you reason with numbers, and (for some degrees) your maths.

The point of them is to tell a university how ready you are for first-year work, separately from your school marks. That cuts both ways: a good NBT can help an application that's borderline on marks, and a weak one can hold back an application that looked strong.

The three tests

AL — Academic Literacy

How you read, interpret and reason with academic text. Almost every university that uses the NBT looks at this one.

QL — Quantitative Literacy

Working with numbers, graphs, tables and everyday quantitative information. Usually written together with AL.

MAT — Mathematics

School-level mathematics. Only required for some degrees, and some faculties explicitly don't use it.

How your result is reported

You don't get a simple percentage. Your result lands in a band, and universities write their requirements in terms of those bands. From lowest to highest:

BandWhat it generally means for your application
BasicThe lowest band. At UCT Humanities, an Academic Literacy result of Basic normally rules an applicant out.
Lower IntermediateAlso normally rules an applicant out of UCT Humanities.
IntermediateThe minimum UCT requires for its Health Sciences degrees.
Upper IntermediateWhat UCT Commerce requires in Academic Literacy and Quantitative Literacy.
ProficientThe top band. UCT's guaranteed-offer band for MBChB needs all NBT domains at Proficient.

Every band requirement in this table is taken from the UCT 2027 Undergraduate Prospectus.

Do you actually have to write it?

This is the part students get wrong most often: it depends on the university and on the degree, not on you. Here is what the universities we've verified so far publish. Anything not listed here is a university whose NBT rules we haven't captured from an official source yet.

University of Cape Town — required for most applicants

  • NBTs are compulsory for South African resident applicants, and for all Health Sciences and Commerce applicants.
  • Commerce: Academic Literacy and Quantitative Literacy must be Upper Intermediate or above. The NBT Maths test is not required for Commerce.
  • Health Sciences: Intermediate or above. Your selection score is out of 900 — your APS out of 600 plus your NBT out of 300. For MBChB, the guaranteed-offer band needs all NBT domains at Proficient.
  • Law: NBTs are compulsory for all LLB applicants. International applicants write the AL test.
  • Humanities: an Academic Literacy result of Lower Intermediate or Basic normally rules an applicant out.
  • Engineering & the Built Environment: you must write NBT Maths, AL and QL — but UCT states the results are not taken into account for EBE admission.
  • Dates: the final NBT date is 3 October 2026, and applications close 31 July 2026.

Source: UCT 2027 Undergraduate Prospectus (uct.ac.za)

Wits — required for Health Sciences and Science

  • Health Sciences: the NBT must be written in person by 17 August. Wits doesn't use APS here at all — it uses a Composite Index of 75% school average across 5 subjects and 25% NBT. Wits does not publish the cut-off score for that index, so nobody can tell you the number you need.
  • Science: the NBT is required.
  • Audiology and Speech-Language Pathology: NBT by 17 August.
  • Architecture: no NBT — but there is an application exercise and an interview.

Source: Wits entry requirements and 2027 course-finder pages (wits.ac.za)

Stellenbosch — mostly not required for 2027

  • NBTs are not required for 2027 entry, except for Law programmes, the School for Tomorrow, AHSD and online-school applicants.
  • LLB: the NBT AQL must be written before 31 July. Selection is 80% school results and 20% NBT, for about 120 places.
  • BCom (Law): the NBT AQL and MAT are required.

Source: SU Admissions Booklet 2027 (su.ac.za)

Rhodes — recommended, not required

Rhodes recommends writing the NBT but does not require it.

Source: Rhodes Undergraduate Prospectus (ru.ac.za)

What this means for your planning

Write it early if there's any chance you need it

The deadlines are real and they're mid-year, well before matric results. Wits Health Sciences closes its NBT window on 17 August; Stellenbosch Law wants the AQL before 31 July; UCT's last date is 3 October 2026.

It can't rescue a subject requirement

If a degree needs Maths at 70% and you have 62%, a great NBT doesn't fix that. Subject minimums and the NBT are separate hurdles — you have to clear both.

This is why some results say "we cannot tell you yet"

In our calculator, degrees that select on NBT show up under "we cannot tell you yet" — because your marks alone genuinely don't decide it, and we won't pretend otherwise.
